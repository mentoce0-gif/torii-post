Mercari Japan is the best secondhand marketplace in the world for the things people collect, and it will not sell you anything.

Not out of spite. It's a domestic service: Japanese addresses, Japanese payment methods, sellers who ship to prefectures rather than countries. Roughly three billion listings have passed through it, and from outside Japan the front door is shut. A proxy is not one way to buy from Mercari; it is the way.

So the question is which one. We checked what four of them charge **for Mercari specifically** — which matters, because two of the four price Mercari differently from everything else they sell — and then costed three realistic carts. The cheapest turned out not to be the one we'd actually use, and we'd rather explain that than quietly recommend around it.

## What Each One Charges for Mercari

All four figures come from the companies' own fee pages, read on 16 August 2026.

| **[ZenMarket](https://zenmarket.jp/en/)** | **¥800 per item** + 1% deposit fee | 60 days | Mercari is its *most expensive* tier |

Two things to notice before any arithmetic.

**Neokyo's ¥350 includes domestic shipping.** That is not a small footnote. Getting a parcel from a Japanese seller to a warehouse runs ¥150–1,500 depending on size and distance, and at every other service on this list it appears on your bill separately. Neokyo folds it in.

**ZenMarket charges more for Mercari than for anything else it sells.** Its standard rate is ¥500, its Recommended Stores rate is ¥300, and its own FAQ says plainly that *"a fee of 800 yen applies to all Mercari items and JDirectItems Auction bids."* If Mercari is where you shop, you are buying at its worst rate.

**Neokyo wins two of the three, and the middle row is not close** — it comes in at under half of the next cheapest. Three items from three different sellers is the most common shape a Mercari cart takes, because Mercari is individuals selling one thing each.

Buyee takes the third row, and only the third row, because its ¥500 covers an order rather than an item. If you've found a seller clearing a collection and you're taking several pieces at once, that structure is worth real money.

ZenMarket loses all three, by a lot. **We can't construct a Mercari cart where it wins.**

## So Why Wouldn't You Just Use Neokyo?

Two reasons, and the first one is disqualifying for some people.

### Neokyo doesn't show you all of Mercari

From their own Mercari page:

> Neokyo filters the best Mercari listings by denying purchases that may be made difficult by picky sellers' requirements.

That is a defensible operational decision — sellers with elaborate conditions cause failed orders, and failed orders cost everyone. But read what it means for you: **the Mercari you see through Neokyo is a subset.** They don't publish how large a subset, and we couldn't establish it.

For most shopping that's invisible and harmless. For collectors it is precisely the wrong trade, because the listing you want is frequently the awkward one — the seller with a wall of conditions, the account with two items and no history, the person who wrote three lines and posted one blurry photo of the exact figure you have been looking for since 2019.

**If a service can't buy the awkward listings, it can't buy the interesting ones.**

### On Mercari, a failed order costs the item, not the fee

This is the thing that makes Mercari different from every other marketplace a proxy touches, and it reframes the whole comparison.

Yahoo Auctions has an end time and a bidding window. Amazon and Rakuten have stock levels and reorders. **Mercari has one of the thing, no bidding, and instant purchase.** First tap wins, and popular listings go in minutes. There is no second chance on that specific item at that specific price — there is only somebody else's listing, later, probably dearer.

So price the risk properly. The gap between the cheapest and second-cheapest service on a single item is **¥300**. The cost of an order that doesn't complete is the item, and on secondhand collectibles the item is frequently irreplaceable.

That is the entire argument for using Mercari's own partner. Buyee is integrated into Mercari directly — listings carry a "Buy with Buyee" button — and that integration is the difference between a purchase that goes through and a purchase that sits in a queue while the seller cancels.

**We can't quantify the difference in failure rates.** Nobody publishes that. What we can say is which direction the risk runs, and that ¥300 is a small stake against a listing you can't replace.

### The split that actually decides it: shops or individuals

There's a distinction inside Mercari that resolves most of the tension above, and Buyee's interface happens to expose it as a filter: **`Individual` sellers against `Shop (mercari Shops)`.**

Mercari Shops are registered businesses selling on the platform. They hold stock, they relist, and if you miss one you can often buy the same thing next week. **Individual sellers are a person clearing a shelf.** One item, no restock, and when it's gone the listing is deleted.

That maps cleanly onto everything above:

- **Buying from a Mercari Shop?** The item is replaceable, so a failed order costs you time rather than the thing. **Optimise on price — Neokyo.**
- **Buying from an individual?** The item is not replaceable. **Optimise on the order completing.**

Most collector purchases on Mercari are the second kind. Most ordinary purchases are the first. If you sort your own buying that way rather than picking one service forever, you'll spend less overall than anyone following a single recommendation.

### One caveat on Neokyo's price advantage: weight

Neokyo's ¥350 is genuinely the lowest service fee here, but its **packing fee scales.** Their published rate is ¥500 for parcels up to 2kg, then **¥500 plus ¥150 for every additional kilogram over 2kg, rounded up.**

Boxed figures reach 2kg quickly — one 1/7 scale in retail packaging is often 1–1.5kg on its own — so a consolidated haul of three or four lands well past that line. A 6kg parcel carries ¥1,100 in packing against ¥500, which eats a meaningful part of the fee advantage.

It doesn't erase it. Three items from three sellers was ¥1,550 at Neokyo against ¥3,450 at Buyee, and ¥600 of packing doesn't close a ¥1,900 gap. **But if you're shipping heavy and buying from few sellers, run your own numbers rather than trusting the headline rate** — which is true of every service on this page.

### Storage, read as an accumulation window

For Mercari the free storage period isn't about waiting out a pre-order. It's about **how long you have to assemble a box** before you're paying to hold it.

**Buyee gives 30 days. Neokyo 45. One Map and ZenMarket 60 each.**

Mercari sellers dispatch fast — often within a day or two — so the clock starts almost immediately after each purchase. If you buy steadily across a month and ship quarterly, Buyee's 30 days is the tightest fit on this list and the one most likely to catch you. If you buy in bursts and ship promptly, it's irrelevant.

## The Authentication Thing Nobody Mentions

Mercari runs its own authentication service on eligible items, and **Buyee surfaces it as a search filter.**

Their Mercari interface carries an `Official Authentication` filter alongside the usual condition and price controls, and authenticated listings show a "With Authentication" badge in results. You can also filter by seller type — `Individual` against `Shop (mercari Shops)` — which separates private sellers from registered businesses.

For anyone buying anything fake-able, that's worth more than the fee difference in a single purchase. Trading cards, branded goods, popular figures with known bootlegs: being able to restrict a search to items Mercari itself has checked is a genuinely different tool from reading a description and hoping.

None of the other three surface it, as far as we could find.

## The Saving That's Bigger Than All of This

Here is the finding we didn't expect, and it makes most of the argument above look like small change.

**Two of these services run percentage-off campaigns on the item price**, in short windows, rotating between marketplaces.

While researching this article on 16 August 2026, One Map was running:

> Mercari JP — **8% OFF** — 2026/08/15 00:00 to 2026/08/16 23:59 (JST)

A forty-eight hour window. Alongside it, a 10% coupon for auctions valid for a single day, and a points draw running the month. ZenMarket, on the same afternoon, was running **9% cashback on Rakuten items** for a four-day window.

Do the arithmetic against everything else on this page. **8% off a ¥20,000 figure is ¥1,600.** The entire spread between the cheapest and most expensive service on a single-item cart is ¥600. **The campaign is worth nearly three times the service choice.**

Which produces an uncomfortable but honest instruction: **before you buy anything expensive on Mercari, open the campaign pages of the services you have accounts with.** If one of them is running a percentage off the item price that week, that is the one to use for that purchase, and the fee tables stop mattering.

We can't tell you when campaigns run. We saw a two-day window and a four-day window on one afternoon, which tells you they're frequent and short and nothing more. **Check rather than assume**, and don't let a guide — including this one — talk you out of ¥1,600 to save ¥300.

## Two Things You Can't Undo

**ZenMarket cannot cancel a Mercari purchase.** Their cancellation fee of ¥1,000 for items already at the warehouse carries an explicit exclusion: *not applicable for items from JDirectItems Auction, Mercari or Rakuma.* Once it's bought, it's yours.

That isn't ZenMarket being awkward. It's how Mercari works — an instant purchase is a completed sale, and there's no auction window to unwind. The same constraint applies in practice everywhere; ZenMarket is just the one that writes it down.

**And nobody is inspecting your item properly.** One Map includes a check that confirms the right item arrived undamaged, and excludes authenticity and anything needing specialist knowledge. ZenMarket excludes inspection entirely. Buyee sells a ¥300 inspection tier with the same ceiling.

**Buy the photos.** On a secondhand marketplace of private individuals, photographs of your specific item before it ships are the only check that catches what actually goes wrong.

## FAQ

**Can I buy from Mercari Japan without a proxy?**

Not from overseas. Mercari Japan is a domestic service — Japanese addresses, Japanese payment methods, sellers shipping domestically. A proxy buys with a Japanese identity and forwards it to you. A forwarding service alone isn't enough either, because you still can't complete the purchase.

**What's the cheapest Mercari proxy?**

Neokyo, at ¥350 per item with domestic shipping included, on every cart we costed except several items from a single seller. Buyee takes that one at ¥500 for the whole order.

**Why is ZenMarket more expensive for Mercari?**

Because Mercari sits in its top fee tier. ZenMarket charges ¥300 at Recommended Stores, ¥500 at most shops, and ¥800 on Mercari and auctions. Add its 1% deposit fee and it's the most expensive option here for this marketplace.

**Is Buyee actually official?**

Yes — Mercari listings carry a "Buy with Buyee" button, and Buyee operates a full Mercari storefront with Mercari's own filters, including authentication status and the individual-versus-shop seller split.

**Can I cancel a Mercari order once it's placed?**

Assume not. ZenMarket states outright that its cancellation option doesn't apply to Mercari, Rakuma or auction items. Mercari purchases complete instantly, so there's no window to unwind.

**How do I avoid buying a fake?**

Filter for Mercari's authentication where it's available, prefer sellers with history, and buy photographs of your specific item before it ships. No proxy's inspection tier checks authenticity — all three that publish limits exclude it explicitly.

**Do these services show all of Mercari?**

Not all of them. Neokyo states it filters out listings from sellers whose requirements make purchases difficult. How much that removes isn't published.

**What if I'm also buying from auctions or shops?**

The answer changes by marketplace, which is the whole problem with one-size guides. ## The Bottom Line

If your Mercari shopping is ordinary goods and the only question is price, **Neokyo is the answer and it isn't close** — ¥350 an item with domestic shipping inside it beats everything here on the carts most people build.

If what you're buying is one-of-a-kind, the calculation changes shape rather than degree. **The stake isn't the ¥300 you save; it's the listing you lose**, and on Mercari there is no second copy at that price. Mercari's own partner, with the full catalogue and the authentication filter, is what we'd point a collector at.

The cleanest way to hold both of those at once is the seller-type split: **Mercari Shops are restockable and should be bought on price; individual sellers are not, and should be bought on certainty.** Sorting your own purchases that way will beat committing to either service for everything.

And whatever you decide between those two, **check the campaign pages before an expensive purchase.** Eight percent off the item price was live for forty-eight hours while we were writing this, and it was worth more than any fee difference on this page. The service fee is the smallest number in this decision. It's just the easiest one to compare.

If you're buying pre-owned figures specifically, condition is the expensive thing to get wrong — [what AmiAmi's pre-owned grades actually mean](/guides/amiami-preowned-grades-explained/) is the calibration we'd read first, and it transfers to reading Mercari listings.
