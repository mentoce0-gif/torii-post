There is a version of this comparison that writes itself, and it is wrong.

It goes: both services charge ¥500, so they cost the same, so pick on features. Every part of that is defensible and the conclusion is still useless, because the ¥500 is attached to different things. Buyee attaches it to your order. One Map attaches it to each item in your order. **Put four figures in a basket and one service charges ¥500 while the other charges ¥2,000.** Split those four figures across four sellers and they charge the same as each other again.

So the useful question isn't which is cheaper. It's what shape your cart is.

## What Each One Actually Charges

Here is the language each company uses, because the wording is the whole story.

**<a href="https://i0c.xyz/dpl/6a7e5186a55c4?p1=vs-onemap-fees&amp;url=https%3A%2F%2Fbuyee.jp%2F%3Flang%3Den" rel="sponsored noopener" target="_blank">Buyee</a> — flat ¥500 per order:**

> Flat rate ¥500 / Per order […] Even if the number of successful bids / purchases are more than 2, it is a flat rate of ¥500. […] Even if multiple purchases are from the same store, it is a flat rate of ¥500.

**[One Map by FROM JAPAN](https://www.fromjapan.co.jp/en/) — 500 yen per item:**

> Product Protection Plan — 500 yen per item
> *If multiple units of the same item are paid together, handling fees will be the same as for one item.

Read that second asterisk carefully, because it is narrower than it first appears. **Three copies of the same figure count as one fee. Three different figures count as three.** The exemption is for quantity, not for variety, and variety is what most collectors actually have in a cart.

There's a second difference sitting inside those two lines. At Buyee the ¥500 buys the purchasing service and nothing else — cover is a separate plan you choose at checkout. At One Map the ¥500 *is* the Product Protection Plan, and their own table lists what comes with it: **60 days of free storage, item consolidation, a guarantee on the domestic leg, a guarantee on the international leg, and a check service.**

That check service matters more than it sounds, so it gets its own section below.

## Four Carts, Costed Both Ways

Domestic shipping is set at ¥650 per seller — the middle of the ¥150–1,500 range Buyee publishes — and applied identically to both, since it depends on the seller rather than the proxy. International shipping is excluded because it depends on your box. Buyee is costed on the free Lite plan unless stated.

| Your cart | Buyee | One Map | Cheaper |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 figure from one shop | ¥1,150 | ¥1,150 | **Tie on price** — see below |
| 4 different figures, **same shop** | **¥1,150** | ¥2,650 | **Buyee by ¥1,500** |
| 3 auction wins, **3 sellers** | ¥3,450 | ¥3,450 | **Exactly level** |
| 3 copies of the **same** figure, one shop | ¥1,150 | ¥1,150 | **Exactly level** |

Two of those four rows are ties, which is the first honest thing to say about this matchup. The services are not competing on price across most of the range. They diverge in precisely one place — **more than one different item bought from the same seller** — and there the gap is large and grows with every item you add.

### The row that decides it for most collectors

Look at row two again. Four different figures from one shop: ¥500 at Buyee, ¥2,000 at One Map.

That is the single most common shape of a serious collector's cart. You find a seller with a good back catalogue, or you're clearing a pre-owned section at AmiAmi or Mandarake, and you take four things at once specifically so they ship together. **Buyee is built for exactly that and One Map charges you for each decision.**

Now look at row three, because it cuts the other way. Three auction wins from three different sellers costs the same at both — ¥500 × 3 either way. If your buying is auctions, where every win is a different person, **the fee structures produce identical bills** and you should choose on something else entirely.

### The single-item row isn't really a tie

Row one shows ¥1,150 at both, and that comparison is not like-for-like.

At One Map, that ¥500 includes the check service. At Buyee, ¥500 buys the purchase and nothing more; **the equivalent inspection is a ¥300 plan**, which takes the same cart to ¥1,450. On matched terms, **One Map is ¥300 cheaper on a single item**, and it is the honest answer for that cart.

Whether you want the inspection is a real question rather than a rhetorical one. On a sealed new figure from a shop, probably not. On a pre-owned listing from an individual seller, it is the difference between finding out in Japan and finding out after you have paid international freight on it.

## What "Checked" Does and Doesn't Mean

One Map is unusually clear about the limits of its check, and the limits are where the useful information is.

What they do: confirm the correct item arrived, and that there is no significant damage.

What they say they do **not** do:

> Checking for subtle flaws or damage, an item's functionality, authenticity, or anything else that requires specialist knowledge is not included in this service.

And a carve-out that collectors should read twice, because it names our exact category:

> For trading cards purchased from private sellers on JDirectItems Auction, Rakuten Rakuma, Mercari JP, etc., items that have been switched, re-shrunk or removed are not covered under our plan, even if the contents differ from the item description.

**So the check catches the wrong box and the crushed box. It does not catch a resealed one.** If your risk is a bootleg or a swapped insert, no plan on either service is buying you protection from it — and anyone telling you otherwise is selling something.

Buyee's ¥300 Inspection plan occupies the same ground with the same ceiling. Neither service is a grading authority. Both are logistics companies that will open a box and look.

Photographs of the item in hand, before it ships, are worth more than any inspection tier on either service, because <em>you</em> are the specialist for your own hobby. A staff member who checks that a figure box is undamaged cannot tell you the paint on the face is off. You can, from a photo, in four seconds.</p></div>

## Storage: 60 Days Against 30

This never appears in fee comparisons because it isn't a fee, and for anyone buying pre-orders it is worth more than the fees are.

**One Map gives 60 days of free storage. Buyee gives 30.** Both then charge daily.

Here's why that lands harder than it reads. Figure release dates move. You reserve something for a March release, it slips to May, and meanwhile you've bought three other things you were planning to ship in the same box. At 30 days those three items start accruing storage while they wait, or you ship them separately and pay a second international freight charge — which costs far more than every service fee on this page combined.

At 60 days you have twice the runway to assemble a box. If your buying is pre-order-shaped, that is the sentence in this article that saves you the most money.

## Where Your Money Actually Goes

Both services bill in two stages and neither can tell you the total up front, because the shipping is measured rather than estimated.

**One Map is explicit about it:** Charge 1 is the item price, paid when you order. Charge 2 arrives after the goods reach the warehouse and get weighed, and bundles domestic shipping, international shipping, the handling fee and any taxes.

**Buyee runs the same shape** — item price and purchase fee first, then domestic and international shipping once the package lands.

Which means the ¥500 either service charges you is, on most orders, a rounding error against the freight. **Choosing a proxy on its service fee alone is optimising the smallest line on the invoice.** The fee matters when it multiplies — which is the entire point of row two above — and stops mattering when it doesn't.

Three things that move the bill more than the fee does:

**Domestic shipping, per seller.** Buyee states it plainly: *"we process each order respectively, even if multiple orders are purchased through the same seller/store. Because of this, there will be separate domestic shipment fees for each order."* Two orders from the same shop means two domestic shipments at both services. If you want one domestic charge, buy in one order.

**The "free shipping" trap.** A Japanese listing marked free delivery often means untracked mail, and both services require trackable methods. The difference lands on your Charge 2. This catches people every time.

**The ¥200,000 line.** Both charge a **¥2,800 customs clearance commission** on packages over ¥200,000 sent via Japan Post services. Identical figure, identical condition, at both companies. Plan the split of an expensive haul around it.

## Where They Aren't Comparable at All

Two structural differences that no fee table captures.

**Buyee is Mercari Japan's official proxy partner.** Mercari listings carry a "Buy with Buyee" button. In practice that means faster processing and fewer orders lost because a seller cancelled while a third-party proxy was still working through a queue. If Mercari is where you shop, that operational advantage is the argument, not the fee.

**One Map lists the secondhand shops as first-class destinations.** Their marketplace menu carries **Surugaya, Mandarake, Lashinban and Komehyo** by name alongside the auction and Mercari entries. For a figure collector that is a meaningful list — Mandarake and Surugaya are where the out-of-print stock actually lives, and having them as native integrations rather than "paste a URL" is a smoother path. Buyee also reaches these shops; the difference is presentation and handling rather than possibility.

One Map also runs a concierge option for listings that are confusing, bundle-only, or need the seller talked to in Japanese. It is slower and it opens doors an automated flow can't.

## What We Couldn't Confirm

Three things we would rather flag than guess at.

**One Map's 8% discount badges.** Their marketplace menu shows an 8% OFF badge against Mercari JP and Surugaya. We could not establish from the fee page what the discount applies to, what the conditions are, or whether it is a standing offer or a campaign. **Do not build a purchase around it without checking it yourself at the time you order.**

**Consolidation pricing.** One Map's plan table marks item consolidation as included. Buyee's own materials say there is no charge for consolidation. Neither page we read gives a standalone price, and we've been caught before publishing a consolidation figure we couldn't stand behind, so we are not publishing one now.

**Buyee's auction wording, still.** Buyee's fee page says a flat ¥500 applies "even if the number of successful bids / purchases are more than 2." Read one way that means all your auction wins in a period cost ¥500 in total, which would make Buyee dramatically cheaper than row three above suggests. Read the other way it merely clarifies that multiple wins inside one order don't stack. **We have costed the conservative reading throughout — ¥500 per seller.** If the generous reading is correct, every Buyee number on this page is too high and row three stops being a tie. Nothing here becomes wrong; Buyee simply wins by more.

**Buyee's "Ship Directly" option** appears on their fee page as a newer delivery method sitting alongside standard shipping. We haven't tested it and won't describe it until we have.

## Which One Should You Use?

| If you're… | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Buying several different items from one seller | **Buyee** | One order, one ¥500, however many items |
| Buying on Mercari | **Buyee** | Official partner — fewer failed orders, not just fees |
| Buying one item you'd like looked at | **One Map** | Inspection is inside the ¥500; Buyee charges ¥300 |
| Waiting out a pre-order | **One Map** | 60 days of storage against 30 |
| Bidding on auctions across many sellers | **Either** | The fees are identical; choose on the two rows above |
| Buying multiples of the same item | **Either** | Same item ×N counts once at both |
| Shopping Mandarake or Surugaya | **One Map**, marginally | Native integrations rather than URL orders |

Most people who buy from Japan regularly end up with an account at both and route each cart to whichever suits its shape. Registration is free at both, and there is no exclusivity — that isn't indecision, it's what the pricing rewards.

## FAQ

**Is One Map the same company as FROM JAPAN?**

Yes. The service now trades as One Map by FROM JAPAN, on the same domain and the same help pages. If you have an older account or an older guide, it's the same service under a new name.

**Which is cheaper, Buyee or One Map?**

Neither, in general. They cost the same on a single item, the same on multiple copies of one item, and the same on items spread across different sellers. Buyee is dramatically cheaper on **several different items bought from one seller**, because its ¥500 covers the order rather than each item.

**Does One Map really charge ¥500 for every item?**

Yes, with one exemption: multiple units of the *same* item paid together count as a single fee. Different items are charged separately, however many arrive in the same box.

**Which one is better for AmiAmi or Mandarake?**

If you're taking one thing, One Map — the shops are native integrations and the fee includes a check. If you're taking several things from the same shop in one order, Buyee, by a wide margin. The [AmiAmi pre-owned grades](/guides/amiami-preowned-grades-explained/) are worth understanding before you buy secondhand from either.

**Can either service protect me from a bootleg?**

No. One Map's check explicitly excludes authenticity and anything requiring specialist knowledge, and names re-shrunk and switched trading cards as outside the plan. Buyee's inspection tier has the same ceiling. Buy photos instead and judge them yourself.

**Do I pay everything up front?**

No, at either. You pay the item price when you order, and shipping and fees once the package has been weighed at the warehouse. Budgeting on the first number is the most common mistake new buyers make.

**What about ZenMarket?**

Different shape again — it bills per item at ¥300, ¥500 or ¥800 depending on the platform, and adds a deposit fee starting at 1%. We've run [ZenMarket against Buyee](/guides/zenmarket-vs-buyee/) head to head, and all five services in the [full comparison](/guides/japan-proxy-shopping-services-compared/).

**Which has better storage?**

One Map, at 60 days free against Buyee's 30. Both charge daily after that. For pre-orders this is worth more than the service fee difference.

## The Bottom Line

These two services are not really competing on price. On three of the four carts we costed, the bills come out level, and the choice has to be made on something else — inspection, storage, Mercari integration, which shops you're actually buying from.

The exception is worth committing to memory, because it's large and it's the cart most collectors have: **more than one different item from the same seller.** Four figures from one shop is ¥500 at Buyee and ¥2,000 at One Map, and no feature on the other side of the ledger closes a ¥1,500 gap.

Everywhere else, the honest advice is to pick on the thing you actually care about. If that's having a human look in the box before it crosses an ocean, or having two months to assemble a shipment, **One Map earns it** — and we'd rather tell you that than pretend one service wins everything.

If you're buying pre-owned rather than new, the condition grades are a separate subject and the more expensive one to get wrong — [what AmiAmi's pre-owned ratings actually mean](/guides/amiami-preowned-grades-explained/) is where we'd start.
