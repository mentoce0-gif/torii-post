You've got a figure in your cart, you're in the United States, and you want to know one number before you commit: what's the shipping going to be?

I went looking for that number in AmiAmi's own documentation, expecting to find a rate table and do some arithmetic. What I found instead was that **AmiAmi publishes rate charts for three shipping methods, and Americans can't use any of them.** The charts cover EMS, Air Parcel and Surface Parcel. The US, since Japan Post stopped accepting most goods parcels to America in August 2025, gets none of those.

So the honest version of this article isn't a price list. It's an explanation of what you can actually use in 2026, what the price quietly includes that it doesn't include for other countries, and the structural reason nobody — including AmiAmi — can hand you a figure before you're invoiced.

If you've come here from a site confidently quoting AmiAmi shipping rates to the US, I'd want to know where they got them.

## What the US Can Actually Use in 2026

AmiAmi maintains a country-by-country table of available shipping methods. Here's what it says for the US, alongside two comparable markets, as of its last update on **30 June 2026**:

| Shipping method | **United States** | United Kingdom | Canada |
|---|---|---|---|
| EMS *(Japan Post)* | **—** | ✅ | ✅ |
| Air Parcel *(Japan Post)* | **—** | ✅ | ✅ |
| Air Small Packet *(Japan Post)* | **—** | ✅ | ✅ |
| Surface Parcel *(Japan Post)* | **—** | ✅ | ✅ |
| DHL Express | **✅** | ✅ | ✅ |
| ECMS | **✅** | — | — |
| Surface Mail Premium | **✅** | — | — |

Look at the shape of that. **Every Japan Post line is dead for the US and alive for the other two.** What America gets instead are two services the UK and Canada can't have at all: Surface Mail Premium, which AmiAmi describes as United States only, and ECMS, which exists for just three destinations worldwide — Hong Kong, South Korea, and the mainland US (the 48 contiguous states, Alaska and Hawaii).

Two consequences follow immediately.

**US territories are worse off than the mainland.** AmiAmi states that shipping to Guam and Puerto Rico is currently unavailable, full stop. If that's you, none of this article helps yet, and the proxy route below is your only path.

**The cheap small-packet trick is gone.** International ePacket Light, AmiAmi's small-parcel option for orders under ¥20,000, is explicitly unavailable for the United States, which AmiAmi attributes to Japan Post's acceptance restrictions on goods to America. For a single keychain, Americans no longer have a cheap postal lane.

## ECMS: The Default Choice Now

ECMS is what most US orders will end up using, and it's worth understanding because it doesn't behave like AmiAmi's other options.

**Transit is 2–4 weeks to the US** — AmiAmi quotes 1–2 weeks for Hong Kong and Korea, so America sits at the slow end of the service. Tracking is included and compensation is full rather than capped.

The unusual part: **ECMS packs and ships your order themselves.** Every other method has AmiAmi packing the box in Japan. With ECMS the carrier does it, which AmiAmi warns "may introduce a slight delay between payment and shipment." So if you've paid and your order sits unshipped for longer than feels right, that's the documented behaviour rather than a problem.

One practical note: claims for loss or damage must be submitted **within four months** of the shipping date. That's shorter than the six months allowed on Japan Post air services, and worth knowing on a service that already takes up to a month to arrive.

## Surface Mail Premium: Two to Three Months, and a US Warehouse

This is the option that tells you something interesting about how AmiAmi operates.

Surface Mail Premium is sea freight. **AmiAmi quotes 2–3 months.** That's not a typo and it's not a worst case — it's the estimate. If you're the kind of collector who preorders eighteen months ahead, another quarter may genuinely not bother you, and this is the cheap lane.

The detail worth knowing before you pick it: **tracking is partial.** In AmiAmi's words, Surface Mail Premium "only has tracking after the shipment has reached our AmiAmi USA warehouse." From the moment it leaves Japan until it lands in America, your order has no individual tracking at all. AmiAmi suggests contacting support 2–4 weeks after payment if you want to know your actual ship date.

That sentence also reveals the mechanism: **AmiAmi runs a US warehouse**, and Surface Mail Premium is a bulk sea shipment into it, followed by domestic delivery. That's why it's US-only, and why the tracking behaves the way it does — for most of the journey your parcel isn't an individual parcel.

## DHL: Fast, Capped, and the One Where You Pay on Arrival

DHL Express is the express option at 3–10 days, with full tracking and On-Demand Delivery features like scheduling and vacation holds. Two hard constraints apply.

**The $800 ceiling.** In April 2025 AmiAmi announced that DHL would no longer be available for US orders with a total merchandise value exceeding roughly $800 USD, because American import rules push those into a formal customs entry that DHL declined to handle. AmiAmi prices in yen, so it converts your order total at the current rate to test the threshold — meaning, as they put it, "the exact eligibility of DHL shipping may vary over time." **The boundary moves with the exchange rate.** A large order simply won't offer you DHL.

**No loose batteries.** DHL won't take rechargeable or dry-cell batteries that aren't built into the product. And DHL requires a complete address in the English alphabet only — no accented characters.

## The Part That Changes the Maths: Import Handling Is Built In

This is the single most useful thing I found, and I haven't seen it explained in an English guide.

For the United States, AmiAmi states that **ECMS and Surface Mail Premium both include import-processing handling in the shipping price**, so nothing is collected when the parcel arrives. DHL is the opposite: the carrier or customs contacts you for import charges on delivery, and delivery can be held until you pay.

| Method | Speed | Who collects import charges |
|---|---|---|
| ECMS | 2–4 weeks | **Nobody — included in the shipping price** |
| Surface Mail Premium | 2–3 months | **Nobody — included in the shipping price** |
| DHL Express | 3–10 days | The carrier, on delivery |

AmiAmi's own FAQ answers the obvious follow-up directly. Asked why shipping costs more for some destinations, they say the price "includes an import-processing handling component." Asked whether you'll pay twice, they say no.

**So "the shipping is expensive" and "the order is expensive" are different complaints, and for US buyers they've quietly merged.** A quoted ECMS price that looks steep next to what a British buyer pays for EMS isn't necessarily a worse deal — the British buyer may still get a bill from customs afterwards, and you won't. Compare totals, not shipping lines.

The flip side is that DHL's shipping quote is the *incomplete* one. It looks competitive at checkout and then arrives with a second invoice attached. If you pick DHL, budget for that separately.

## Why AmiAmi Can't Quote You Before You Order

Now the structural answer, which is more useful than any number would have been.

AmiAmi states plainly that **shipping is calculated after the order is placed and every item in it is released and ready to ship.** Not at checkout — after. I looked at a live AmiAmi cart while writing this, and it's exactly as described: the cart totals the merchandise and shows no shipping line at all.

For in-stock items you can ask. AmiAmi will provide an estimate if you open a support ticket with the shop code of the item, though they cap that at **three or four items** for a combined order.

For pre-orders, they won't — and the reason isn't reluctance:

> we are unable to provide shipping estimates for pre-order items because we do not have the measurements/weight of the items prior to its release date

The figure doesn't exist yet. Neither does its box. Nobody at AmiAmi knows how big the carton will be, so no honest number can be given. Their suggested workaround is genuinely the best available: **send them the shop code of a comparable item that has already been released** and ask for that instead.

Which lands you on the thing that makes this uncomfortable in practice. The first time you see your real shipping cost is in the Payment Request email — and that email starts a **seven-day payment deadline that can't be extended**. If you've budgeted the figure but not the freight, you find out and have a week to solve it. I've written up [how that deadline works and what happens if you miss it](/guides/amiami-preorder-payment-cancellation/), because the penalty is harsher than most people expect.

There's a trap hidden in that overlap. The natural reaction to an unexpectedly large shipping quote is to change something — switch to a slower method, split the order, combine it with another. You can do all of that from Modify Existing Orders. But AmiAmi warns that **any modification requires processing on their side, and you cannot proceed to payment while it's in progress**, which is why they advise against modifying an order close to its deadline. So the moment you're most tempted to restructure your order is the moment it's least safe to. If the quote is survivable, pay it and restructure next time.

## What You Can Actually Control

You can't control the rate. You can control four things that move the number.

**Keep orders to one release month.** AmiAmi only combines items releasing in the same month — and the cart enforces it, with a per-item dropdown offering "combine within this order," "ship separately," or "combine with orders from the same month." Two figures from different months means two boxes and two shipping charges, whatever you'd prefer.

**Don't add a cheap item to a preorder to "make shipping worthwhile."** It doesn't ship any sooner and it can't be invoiced until the slow item lands. All you've done is delay the cheap thing by a quarter.

**Think in box size, not item price.** International carriers generally bill on whichever is greater, actual weight or volumetric weight — DHL publishes its own formula as length × width × height in centimetres divided by 5,000. AmiAmi's help pages don't state how their quotes are computed, so I won't put words in their mouth, but the practical upshot is the same everywhere in this industry: **a large light box costs more than its weight suggests.** Scale figure packaging is mostly air.

**Pick the lane that matches your patience, then stop thinking about it.** If you want it now and the order is under $800, DHL and a customs bill. If you want it reasonably and predictably, ECMS. If you genuinely don't care when it arrives, Surface Mail Premium and check back in a season.

One asymmetry to file away in case something goes wrong: the window for filing a loss or damage claim differs sharply by method. AmiAmi gives **one month from the shipping date for DHL**, four months for ECMS, and six months for Japan Post air services. Damage claims are tighter still — one week from delivery, whichever method you used. The fastest service has the shortest memory, so open the box promptly and photograph problems the day they arrive.

## FAQ

**How much is AmiAmi shipping to the US?**
There's no published answer. AmiAmi's rate charts cover EMS, Air Parcel and Surface Parcel, none of which currently ship to the US, and it publishes no chart for DHL, ECMS or Surface Mail Premium. Your figure is calculated when the order is invoiced.

**Can I get a quote before I order?**
For in-stock items, yes — open a support ticket with the item's shop code, up to three or four items. For pre-orders, no: AmiAmi says the measurements and weight don't exist before release. Ask about a comparable released item instead.

**Why is shipping to the US more expensive than to Europe?**
Partly because it includes something Europe's doesn't. For ECMS and Surface Mail Premium, import processing is built into the price, so nothing is collected on delivery. Compare the total landed cost, not the shipping line.

**Will I get a customs bill?**
Not with ECMS or Surface Mail Premium, per AmiAmi's own statement that nothing is collected at delivery. With DHL, yes — the carrier collects import charges on arrival.

**Why can't I select DHL?**
Most likely your order's merchandise total exceeds roughly $800 USD, which since April 2025 makes DHL unavailable for US shipments. The threshold is converted from yen at the current rate, so it shifts. Loose batteries and address-format problems can also remove it.

**Is EMS really gone for the US?**
It's not listed as available for the United States in AmiAmi's country table as of 30 June 2026, along with Air Parcel and Air Small Packet. That follows Japan Post's suspension of most goods parcels to America from August 2025.

**Does Surface Mail Premium really take three months?**
2–3 months is AmiAmi's own estimate. And you won't have tracking for most of it — tracking begins once the shipment reaches AmiAmi's US warehouse.

**Can they mark it as a gift to reduce duty?**
No. AmiAmi states this is against Japanese law, and ships everything marked "Merchandise" at full declared value.

**My shipping method changed without me asking. Why?**
AmiAmi reserves the right to modify your shipping method without prior notice if your order isn't eligible for what you selected — usually a size, weight, or item-restriction issue.

## The Bottom Line

For US buyers in 2026, AmiAmi is a three-lane road: DHL if it's small enough and you want it this week, ECMS if you want the sensible middle, Surface Mail Premium if you've made peace with waiting a season. The Japan Post options everyone's old guide describes are not available to you.

Two things are worth carrying away. **The price on the first two already contains your import handling**, which makes them look worse than they are next to other countries' quotes. And **no number exists until the invoice**, so the discipline that actually protects you is budgeting for freight when you place the order rather than when you're asked to pay for it.

If you're buying pre-owned rather than new, the grades are their own subject — [what AmiAmi's pre-owned ratings actually mean](/guides/amiami-preowned-grades-explained/) is where I'd start, since a B-grade figure at 40% off changes the shipping arithmetic considerably.
