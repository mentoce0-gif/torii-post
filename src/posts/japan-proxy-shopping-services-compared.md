So you finally found it. That out-of-print figure, that limited-edition vinyl, that perfect secondhand Seiko on Mercari Japan — and then you hit the wall every overseas buyer eventually hits: *"This seller does not ship internationally."*

I've been buying from Japanese sites for years, and I can tell you the wall is real, but it's also completely climbable. Japanese online marketplaces — Mercari, Yahoo! Auctions, Rakuten, Surugaya, AmiAmi's pre-owned section — are treasure troves that most sellers simply never bother opening up to international buyers. Not because they don't want your money, but because international shipping paperwork is a hassle and most individual sellers won't touch it.

That's where proxy shopping services come in. A proxy buys the item for you with a Japanese address and payment method, receives it at their warehouse, and ships it to your door. Simple concept — but the fees, shipping options, and quality of service vary wildly between companies, and picking the wrong one for your situation can quietly add 20–30% to your total cost.

I've used all five of the services below. Here's the honest breakdown, including who each one is actually right for.

## How Proxy Shopping Actually Works (60-Second Version)

Every service in this list follows the same basic flow:

1. **You find an item** on a Japanese site (or search directly inside the proxy's own portal, which is easier).
2. **The proxy buys it** using their Japanese address and payment method. You pay the item price + their service fee.
3. **It arrives at their warehouse**, usually within 2–5 days. Most services photograph it on request.
4. **You choose international shipping** — and this is where consolidation matters. If you've bought five items, shipping them in one box instead of five can cut your shipping bill in half.
5. **It ships to you.** Expect 4–10 days for express options (DHL, FedEx, EMS) or 1–3 months for surface mail.

The service fee is only part of the picture. Consolidation policy, domestic shipping handling, and currency conversion rates are where the sneaky costs hide. Keep that in mind as you read the reviews below.

## The Four Hidden Costs That Decide Your Real Total

Before we get into the individual reviews, let me save you from the mistakes I made in my first year of proxy shopping. The advertised service fee is the *least* important number. These four costs are what actually separate a good deal from a bad one:

**1. Domestic shipping.** Every item has to travel from the seller to the proxy's warehouse first. Buyee publishes a range of ¥150–1,500 per shipment depending on size, origin and method, and the others land in similar territory; ¥500–1,000 is the band most orders actually fall into. Buy five items from five different sellers and you could rack up ¥4,000 in domestic shipping before your haul even leaves Japan. This is the single most common "wait, why is my invoice so high?" moment for beginners. (It's also why Neokyo bundling domestic shipping into its flat fee is a bigger deal than it sounds.)

**2. Dimensional weight.** International carriers charge by actual weight *or* box volume, whichever is greater. A big box of light plushies gets billed like a box of bricks. Packing quality varies noticeably between services — a snug repack can save you thousands of yen on a large order, and an oversized box can silently erase every yen you saved on fees.

**3. Currency conversion.** Some services apply their own exchange rate with a built-in spread of 2–4% above the market rate. On a ¥50,000 order, that's up to ¥2,000 vanishing invisibly. Paying in yen with a no-foreign-fee credit card, where the service allows it, is usually the cleanest option.

**4. Storage deadlines.** Free storage windows range from 30 to 60 days depending on the service. That's plenty for normal shopping — until you're holding three items waiting for a pre-order that ships next season. Miss the deadline and you're either paying storage fees or forced to ship a half-empty box.

None of these are scams; they're just the physics and economics of moving boxes across the ocean. But once you know to check all four, comparing services gets a lot less confusing.

## ZenMarket — Strongest on Ordinary Shops, Weakest on Auctions

[ZenMarket](https://zenmarket.jp/en/) was our default recommendation for years, on the strength of a flat ¥300 fee. **That flat fee no longer exists**, and re-checking it changed our answer to this whole question.

ZenMarket's fee now depends on where the item came from:

| Where you bought it | Fee per item |
|---|---|
| A ZenMarket Recommended Store | ¥300 |
| Amazon, Rakuten, most other shops | ¥500 |
| **Mercari and JDirectItems Auction** | **¥800** |

The ¥800 tier is the one that hurts, because auctions and Mercari are exactly what most collectors are here for. Three auction wins cost ¥2,400 in fees before anything ships. There is also a **funds deposit fee starting at 1%**, which their own summary lists as always payable — so ZenMarket is not the pure flat-fee service the internet still describes.

Where it remains genuinely strong is everything that isn't the headline number. **Consolidation is free.** Storage is free for **60 days** — double Buyee's — which matters more than it sounds when you're holding items waiting on a pre-order. Shipping insurance for parcels under ¥5,000,000 is included rather than sold as an add-on. And buying several units of the *same* item costs one fee, not one per unit.

The buying experience is the smoothest here too: integrated search across auctions, Mercari, Rakuten and thousands of shops, auto-translated listings that are actually readable, reliable auction sniping, and an unusually wide range of payment methods including PayPal and crypto.

**The downsides:** the ¥800 auction and Mercari tier, the deposit fee, and a hard rule that **auction, Mercari and Rakuma purchases cannot be cancelled for any reason.** Support is polite but slow during sale seasons.

**Choose ZenMarket if:** you buy from ordinary shops rather than auctions, you need items held longer than a month, or you're buying multiples of the same thing.

## Buyee — Strongest on Auctions, Mercari, and One-Shop Hauls

Buyee is the biggest name in the game, and it has one structural advantage nobody can match: **it's the official proxy partner of Mercari Japan.** Mercari listings literally show a "Buy with Buyee" button. That integration means faster processing and fewer cancelled orders on Mercari than any competitor.

Buyee's purchase fee is **¥500 flat per order — and it does not matter how many items are in that order.** Their own fees page is unusually blunt about it: even if you win two or more auctions, or buy several things from the same store, it stays ¥500.

That single sentence decides everything about when Buyee is cheap. The item price is irrelevant — a ¥2,000 CD and a ¥200,000 watch cost the same ¥500 to buy. What matters is how many items you can get into one order. Buy one thing and you've paid ¥500 where ZenMarket would charge ¥300. Buy four things from the same shop and you've paid ¥500 where ZenMarket would charge ¥1,200. **The crossover is two items: from the second item in an order onwards, Buyee is ahead.**

The catch is the other side of the same coin. Domestic shipping is billed per order too, and Buyee is explicit that separate orders are processed separately "even if multiple orders are purchased through the same seller." So four items from four different shops means four ¥500 fees *and* four domestic shipments — the worst case on this entire list.

The platform itself is polished, with apps that actually work, frequent coupon campaigns (their shipping discount coupons can be genuinely substantial), and the widest marketplace coverage in the industry.

**The downsides:** There is a guarantee plan attached to every order, and the one Buyee recommends costs ¥500. The free "Lite" tier exists and you can select it, but it is not the default — that's ¥500 per order that many buyers pay without noticing. Their packing also has a reputation for being generously — let's say *spaciously* — boxed, which inflates dimensional weight charges. And free storage runs 30 days, the shortest here, with daily fees after that up to a 90-day ceiling.

We could not confirm Buyee's package consolidation fee. Their FAQ says the service exists, has no application deadline, and works across different shops, but every page we could reach declines to name a price. Our July table said ¥500 per package; we can no longer stand behind that figure, so we've stopped asserting it rather than repeat it.

**Choose Buyee if:** you shop primarily on Mercari, or you can group two or more items into a single order from one shop. Avoid it when your cart is one item, or when it's spread thinly across many different sellers — that's the pattern its pricing punishes hardest.

## FROM JAPAN — Flat per Item, Whatever the Item Costs

FROM JAPAN has been around since the early days of proxy shopping. We previously described its fee as ¥300 per item with an ¥800 per-store cap. **Re-checking in August 2026, we can't find support for either figure.** Their own buying guide gives one number: a **Product Protection Plan at ¥500 per item**, described as applying to every purchase rather than as an upsell. If a per-store ceiling still exists, it isn't published anywhere we can reach.

What survives the correction is the shape of the pricing. The fee doesn't scale with what you paid, so a ¥50,000 watch and a ¥500 keychain cost the same ¥500 to buy — and buying several units of the *same* item in one transaction is charged once, not per unit. Against ZenMarket's ¥800 on auctions and Mercari, that flat ¥500 is the cheaper answer for auction-heavy carts spread across sellers, which is precisely where Buyee's per-order pricing falls apart.

Their "Buy for me" concierge option is also worth knowing about. For listings that are confusing, bundle-only, or require seller communication, their staff will handle the back-and-forth in Japanese. It's slower, but it opens doors the automated services can't.

**The downsides:** The two-step payment system (pay for the item first, then pay fees + shipping later) confuses first-timers and means two separate currency conversions. The website feels a generation older than ZenMarket's or Buyee's. Processing can be slower, which matters for auction wins with impatient sellers.

**Choose FROM JAPAN if:** your cart is auction or Mercari items spread across several sellers — the one pattern that's expensive at both ZenMarket and Buyee — or you need human help with a tricky listing.

## Neokyo — Strongest When You Hate Fee Surprises

Neokyo is the smallest service on this list, but it's earned a loyal following among figure and doujin collectors for one reason: **radical fee transparency.** The ¥350 per-item fee *includes* domestic shipping to their warehouse — a cost that every other service passes through separately and that catches beginners off guard constantly.

That means when you're calculating a Neokyo order, the math is genuinely simple: item price + ¥350 + international shipping. No surprise ¥800 domestic shipping charge appearing after purchase. For budget-conscious buyers who hate fee anxiety, this is worth a lot. Consolidation is free, with a ¥500 packing fee for packages under 2kg.

Their support team is small but responsive, and the whole service has a pleasant "run by people who actually collect things" energy.

**The downsides:** The marketplace search is less polished than the big players', with rougher translations. Some payment options are limited. And because the team is small, processing times can stretch during Golden Week or year-end sale rushes. No real concierge service for complicated purchases.

**Choose Neokyo if:** you want zero fee surprises, you're a collector making regular small-to-medium hauls, or you've been burned by hidden domestic shipping charges elsewhere.

## Japan Rabbit — For the Purchases Nobody Else Can Make

Japan Rabbit (you may know it by its old name, White Rabbit Express) is a different animal entirely. It's not a self-service platform — it's a concierge. You send them a link, or even just a description, and a human in Tokyo figures out how to get it.

That includes stores that actively block proxy services, fan club exclusives, lottery items, in-store pickups, and purchases that require phone calls or negotiation in Japanese. I've seen them pull off purchases that every automated service flat-out rejected.

Pricing is quote-based and unapologetically premium. You're paying for human time, and it shows in both the cost and the quality — packing is excellent, communication is in fluent English, and edge cases get solved instead of auto-cancelled.

**The downsides:** cost, obviously. For routine Mercari or Rakuten purchases, using Japan Rabbit is like hiring a private chef to make toast. This is a specialist tool.

**Choose Japan Rabbit if:** the item is rare, the store blocks proxies, or the purchase requires a human being on the ground in Japan.

## A Real-World Example: The Same Cart, Three Different Bills

Numbers make this concrete. Say you're buying a typical collector's haul: a ¥8,000 figure from AmiAmi, a ¥3,500 manga lot from Mercari, and a ¥2,000 CD from Yahoo! Auctions — three items, three different sellers.

- **ZenMarket:** ¥2,100 in service fees — the AmiAmi item is an ordinary shop at ¥500, but the Mercari lot and the auction CD are ¥800 each. Free consolidation, plus ~1% on the deposit.
- **Buyee:** ¥1,500 in purchase fees — three separate orders at ¥500 each, because the items come from three different sellers — and a roomier box that can nudge up the international rate.
- **FROM JAPAN:** ¥1,500 in fees (¥500 × 3), free consolidation.

All three pay domestic shipping on each of the three sellers, so that cancels out. **Buyee and FROM JAPAN tie at ¥1,500. ZenMarket is the most expensive at ¥2,100**, entirely because two of the three items came from platforms it charges ¥800 for.

Now change one thing and watch it move again. Buy all three from the *same ordinary shop* instead. ZenMarket falls to ¥1,500 (3 × ¥500). FROM JAPAN stays at ¥1,500, since its fee is per item and doesn't care who sold it. **Buyee falls to ¥500 and wins outright**, because one order is one fee no matter what's in it — and you drop from three domestic shipments to one, which saves more than the service fees did.

Nothing about the prices changed in either version. What changed was *where* the items came from and *how many sellers* were involved. That, not the value of your cart, is what decides this.

The lesson: don't pick a service based on one comparison table (including mine). Picture your *typical* cart, run the math once, and the right answer usually becomes obvious.

## Five Questions That Pick Your Service

If you skipped the reviews, this is the short version. Answer these in order and stop at your first yes.

**1. Is most of your cart from auctions or Mercari?**
→ **Buyee.** This is the single biggest fork in the road. ZenMarket charges ¥800 per item on both platforms; Buyee charges ¥500 for the whole order. Three auction wins: ¥2,400 against ¥500. Buyee is also Mercari's official proxy partner, so processing is faster and fewer orders fall through.

**2. Are two or more items coming from the same shop?**
→ **Buyee**, for the structural reason above: one order, one fee. Four items from one shop costs ¥500 at Buyee against ¥2,000 at FROM JAPAN and ¥2,000 at ZenMarket if that shop happens to be Mercari. Nothing else here rewards grouping.

**3. Buying single items from several different ordinary shops?**
→ **ZenMarket**. This is the pattern that punishes Buyee hardest — every seller is a separate ¥500 *and* a separate domestic shipment. ZenMarket charges ¥500 an item at ordinary shops, and ¥300 at its Recommended Stores, which is the cheapest per-item rate anywhere in this guide. FROM JAPAN matches it at ¥500 but has no ¥300 tier.

**4. Have you been burned by a domestic shipping charge you didn't expect?**
→ **Neokyo.** Its ¥350 per item already includes the trip to the warehouse. Since a domestic shipment runs ¥500–1,000 and you pay one per seller, a cart from three different sellers can cost ¥2,000 more elsewhere before anything leaves Japan. The trade is a rougher search experience and slower processing during sale rushes.

**5. Waiting on a pre-order, or holding items to fill a box?**
→ **ZenMarket**, on storage alone. Sixty days free against Buyee's thirty, and this is the one axis where the answer doesn't depend on what you're buying.

**And if the shop refuses proxies outright** — some do — none of the above will help. That's [Japan Rabbit](https://japanrabbit.com/)'s entire reason to exist, at concierge prices.

## Which International Shipping Method Should You Pick?

Once your box is packed, every service offers roughly the same menu of carriers, and the right choice depends on what's inside:

- **DHL / FedEx express** — 3–7 days, full tracking, best for anything valuable or fragile. Costs the most upfront but handles customs paperwork smoothly, which can actually make it *cheaper* in tears and delays for high-value orders.
- **EMS (Japan Post express)** — the classic middle option: 4–10 days to most countries, solid tracking, decent insurance, usually cheaper than DHL for small-to-medium boxes.
- **Airmail / small packet** — 1–3 weeks, fine for books, CDs, and anything under 2kg that won't break your heart if it dawdles.
- **Surface (sea) mail** — 1–3 months, but often a third of the airmail price. Genuinely great for heavy, non-urgent hauls like manga box sets. I've sent 15kg of books by sea for less than a 5kg airmail box would have cost.

One practical tip: don't default to the fastest option out of nerves. For a typical figure or clothing order, EMS hits the sweet spot of speed, cost, and insurance coverage almost every time.

## FAQ

**Is proxy shopping from Japan legit, or will I get scammed?**
The five services above are all established companies that have operated for years and processed millions of orders. The genuine risks are item condition on secondhand marketplaces (read listings carefully, request warehouse photos) and customs charges on your end — not the proxies themselves.

**How much does shipping from Japan actually cost?**
As a rough guide in 2026: a 1kg box runs about ¥2,500–4,500 via airmail-class options and ¥5,000+ via DHL/FedEx express, depending on destination. This is exactly why consolidation matters more than service fees for multi-item orders.

**Do I have to pay customs duties?**
Depends on your country's de minimis threshold. US buyers currently face duties on most shipments, EU buyers pay VAT on virtually everything, and thresholds change — check your country's current rules before a big order rather than relying on year-old forum posts.

**Which service is cheapest overall?**
There isn't one, and the honest answer is shorter than you'd like: check where you're buying from. Auctions and Mercari go to Buyee, because ZenMarket charges ¥800 an item on those two platforms and Buyee charges ¥500 for the order. Ordinary shops go to ZenMarket or FROM JAPAN. Several items from a single seller go to Buyee or FROM JAPAN. It depends on the shape of your cart, not what it cost.

**Can proxies buy pre-orders and lottery items?**
Pre-orders: yes, all the major services handle them, though your storage clock and payment timing vary. Lottery (chuusen) items and fan club exclusives: usually only via concierge services like Japan Rabbit.

**How long does the whole process take?**
Typical timeline: 2–5 days for the item to reach the warehouse, then 4–10 days international express shipping. Budget two to three weeks door-to-door for a normal order, longer if you're consolidating multiple purchases.

**Can I use a proxy to buy from Amazon Japan?**
Usually you don't need to — Amazon Japan ships many items internationally itself, and its English interface is decent. Proxies earn their keep on the marketplaces that *don't* ship overseas: Mercari, Yahoo! Auctions, Rakuten sellers, Surugaya, and small specialty shops. Check Amazon's own international shipping first and save the proxy fee when you can.

**What happens if my item arrives at the warehouse damaged or wrong?**
This is where service quality really shows. All five services will photograph items on request (sometimes for a small fee) — use that option for anything secondhand or fragile. If a marketplace seller misrepresented an item, the proxy can usually open a dispute on your behalf, but windows are short: check your warehouse photos within a few days, not weeks. For genuinely valuable purchases, this dispute support is a strong argument for concierge-style services.

## The Bottom Line

The "seller does not ship internationally" wall stopped being a real obstacle years ago — it's now just a routing problem. What's left is a pricing problem, and it has a short answer: **look at where your items are coming from before you pick a service.** Auctions and Mercari push you toward Buyee; ordinary shops push you toward ZenMarket or FROM JAPAN; a long wait for a pre-order pushes you toward ZenMarket's 60-day storage regardless of the rest.

Whichever you pick, [start with a small test order](https://zenmarket.jp/en/) before trusting a service with a ¥40,000 grail. You'll learn the interface, see the real fees on an invoice, and shop with confidence after that.

Happy hunting — the treasure trove is bigger than you think.
