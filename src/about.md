<h1>About Torii Post</h1>
<div class="page-body">

Most English-language guides about buying from Japan are written by the companies selling you the service. Search "how to buy from Surugaya" or "best proxy service" and nearly every result is a proxy company's own blog, politely concluding that — surprise — you should use them.

Torii Post exists to be the other thing: an independent guide, written from inside Japan, by people who actually use these shops, marketplaces, and services with their own money.

## What we cover

- **Buying from Japan** — proxy services, marketplaces like Mercari and Yahoo! Auctions, specialty shops for figures, music, fashion, and parts, and the real costs of getting a box across the ocean.
- **Traveling in Japan** — the practical, unglamorous questions that official tourism sites skip, answered with local knowledge.

## How we work

We never accepted payment for coverage or rankings. This site did carry affiliate links until 23 August 2026; **they have all been removed**, and the record of how they worked is in our [affiliate disclosure](/affiliate-disclosure/). Recommendations are built from operators' own published terms, our own arithmetic, and the documented experience of the communities we're part of — each named as what it is. When something has a real downside, we say so in plain language.

## This site is archived

**Torii Post stopped publishing on 23 August 2026.** The guides are left online because the reasoning in them is still worth reading, but nothing here is being updated: fees change, shops change their policies, and no one is checking any more. Treat every number as accurate *as of the date on the guide* and verify it at the source before you act on it.

If we got something wrong, we still want to know — [email us](mailto:hello@toriipost.com). Corrections are read, even though the guides are no longer revised.
